# 1. What Is This Platform? (No Code Yet)

Before looking at a single line of code, let's understand the product the way a user does.

## The problem it solves

Preparing for a software engineering job interview (at companies like Google, Amazon, Meta) usually means:
practicing coding problems, designing systems on a whiteboard, and answering behavioral questions — normally
with a friend, a paid mentor, or nobody at all. **OnePoint AI is a website that plays the role of that
practice partner**, using AI models as the "interviewer," available any time, for any of the four common
interview types.

## The four interview types

| Type | What's practiced | Example |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| **DSA** (Data Structures & Algorithms) | Coding problems, the kind asked on LeetCode | "Find the two numbers in this array that add up to a target." |
| **System Design** | Designing a large-scale system on a (virtual) whiteboard | "Design a URL shortener that handles a million requests a day." |
| **LLD** (Low-Level Design) | Designing classes and objects for a smaller system | "Design the classes for a parking lot." |
| **Managerial / Behavioral** | Questions about past work experience | "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate." |

Each of these also has a **Tutor** version (`tutorDsa`, `tutorLld`, `tutorSystemDesign`) — instead of a strict,
timed interview, the AI teaches the topic step by step, at the user's pace, with no score and no clock.

## What a user can actually do on the site

1. **Sign in** with Google or an email/password account.
2. **Start a mock interview**: pick a type, a difficulty, a target company (optional), a programming language,
   and which AI model plays the interviewer. A timer starts, and the AI presents a problem and has a back-and-forth
   conversation with the candidate, including a real code editor for coding rounds.
3. **End the interview** and receive a **scorecard**: a score out of 100, a hire/no-hire verdict, and detailed
   written feedback on strengths and weaknesses — generated by another AI pass over the full transcript.
4. **Review History**: every past session (finished or not) is listed, grouped by type, with the transcript,
   the scorecard, and optional AI-generated revision notes.
5. **Work through the DSA Master Sheet**: a curated checklist of 322 must-solve LeetCode problems organized
   by pattern (hashing, sliding window, DP, etc.), with progress tracking, personal notes, and a one-click
   "practice this problem" button that starts a mock interview on exactly that problem.
6. **Follow a Prep Plan**: a week-by-week study calendar.
7. **Run an Interview Loop**: several mock interview rounds back-to-back (like a real "interview loop" at a
   company), tracking pass/fail per round.
8. **Request access** if new signups are limited, and (for admins) approve or deny those requests, ban/suspend
   users, adjust quotas, and toggle platform-wide settings like maintenance mode — all from an **Admin panel**.

## Who uses it, and how access is controlled

Every account starts in a **free trial** (a handful of AI-backed sessions for free). After that, an admin must
approve the account for continued daily use, or the person can request access. This is explained fully in
[05_authentication_and_access_control.md](05_authentication_and_access_control.md).

## Why AI, and why does it matter that there are multiple "models"?

The "interviewer" and the "scorer" are Large Language Models (LLMs) — the same category of technology as
ChatGPT. This platform doesn't build its own AI; it sends carefully written instructions ("prompts") to
existing AI providers (Groq, Google Gemini, OpenRouter) and streams their reply back to the user, word by
word, as it's generated. Because relying on a single AI provider is risky (it can be slow, hit a quota, or go
down), the backend can **automatically fall back** to a different model if the first one fails — the user
never has to notice. The full mechanics are in [07_ai_integration_explained.md](07_ai_integration_explained.md).
